# Reports

## Audience

A portfolio manager or head of risk on a multi-asset desk, someone who can act on the
finding by resizing exposure or adding a hedge, and who needs the reasoning behind a
number rather than just the number.

## Format, and why

`final_report.md` is a written report rather than a deck or a dashboard, and the choice
follows from what the finding actually is.

The central result comes with a caveat that changes how it should be used: the monitor
scores poorly against the crisis windows it was originally aimed at, and the reason is
that two of those three windows were not diversification breakdowns at all. A number and
the reason it should not be read the obvious way have to sit in the same place, in order,
or the reader takes away the wrong conclusion. Slides separate those into different
bullets on different pages and invite exactly that mistake.

A dashboard would be right if the question were exploratory, if a reader needed to try
different windows or different asset pairs and see what changed. Here the analysis is
fixed and the argument is sequential, so an interactive tool would add controls nobody
needs and lose the thread that makes the result interpretable.

## Contents

- `final_report.md` is the deliverable: executive summary, three findings with charts,
  assumptions and risks, a sensitivity table, and decision implications.
- `images/` holds the figures, all generated by `notebooks/project_pipeline.ipynb`.
  - `annual_hedge_and_dr.png`, the stock-bond correlation and diversification ratio by year
  - `stress_measures.png`, the four stress measures with crisis windows shaded
  - `regime_spytlt.png`, the correlation with the fitted regime and thresholds
  - `eda_prices_vol.png`, cumulative growth and rolling volatility for the basket
- `project_pipeline.html` is the fully executed notebook exported for reading without a
  Python environment.
